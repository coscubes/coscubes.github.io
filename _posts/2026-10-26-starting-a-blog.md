For years my corner of the internet was a single `index.html` with a list of papers on it.
It did the one job I needed at the time: if someone wanted a PDF, it was there. But a
publication list is a record of finished work, and almost everything interesting happens
before the work is finished.

So: a blog.

<!--more-->

## What I expect to write about

Mostly the things I spend my days on, which cluster around video understanding:

- **Paper notes.** Not summaries — summaries are what abstracts are for. I mean the
  version where I try to explain why a method works, discover I can't, and go back and
  read it properly.
- **Learning from limited labels.** Video annotation is miserable and expensive, which
  makes it the most honest constraint in the field.
- **Representations for action recognition.** The question that got me into research in
  the first place: what exactly should a model keep from a sequence of frames?
- **Things that went wrong.** Negative results are badly under-published, and I find them
  more useful than the alternative when I'm the one debugging at 2am.

## A note on cadence

I'm not going to promise a schedule. Blogs that promise schedules tend to produce one
apologetic post about the schedule and then nothing. I'd rather write when I have
something worth the reader's time.

If you want to follow along, there's an [RSS feed]({{ '/feed.xml' | relative_url }}).
If you'd rather just talk, my email is in the footer — I read everything, and I'm
always interested in collaborations.
